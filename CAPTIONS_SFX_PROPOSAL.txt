RIXU CAPTIONS + SFX ASSISTANT
Current proposal - 28 October 2026
Status: Historical design. Final user requirements and the development build are documented in README.md and VALIDATION.md; they supersede provisional choices below.

PRODUCT DIRECTION

Your main workflow is captioning and using sound effects from your own library.
Make these the first release. General timeline editing remains a supporting
capability and a possible later expansion.

The proposed panel combines caption editing, searchable personal SFX, and
natural-language directions for placing sounds at specific words or moments.
Start with your installed Premiere 26.2.0 and retain the planned modern/legacy
connectors for a tested range of other versions.

USER-SPECIFIED SETTINGS
- Caption outputs: both standard subtitles and animated graphic captions.
- SFX behavior: automatically choose/place sounds using your instructions.
- Library root: D:\Asset\SFX, including all nested folders.
- Dialogue: English and Hinglish.
- Provisional writing choice: Roman-script Hindi mixed with English for Hinglish;
  preserve code switching rather than translating everything into English.

1. THE WORKFLOW

Select a finished sequence or marked range -> get a timed transcript -> correct
and group captions -> choose a style -> identify sound cues -> search your
library recursively -> automatically place matching sounds -> review playback
and optionally audition replacements.

Example instruction, illustrating configurable preferences:
'Use my short-caption style. Add a soft pop on the three marked keywords and
a whoosh ending at this transition. Use only my library and keep the sounds
under the dialogue.'

Caption text, words, markers and cut locations share one timing map. A sound
can follow a particular spoken word, a graphic entrance, a marker or a cut.
A later timing change marks dependent cues for re-analysis; it must not silently
move sounds whose intended anchors are no longer clear.

2. CAPTIONS MODULE

Input:
- Reuse an existing transcript when accessible, or obtain transcription from
  the selected dialogue audio through a configured transcription provider.
- Import timed captions/transcripts when already available.
- Resolve which audio track represents the intended dialogue.

Editing:
- Word timing and editable caption blocks.
- Spelling, names and punctuation corrections with changes visible for review.
- Phrase grouping, words per block, maximum lines, gap/minimum-duration settings.
- Keep the spoken meaning; do not silently paraphrase the dialogue.
- Allow a project dictionary and reusable caption presets.

Output has two distinct options:
A. Standard subtitle captions: timed text with a native caption/SRT workflow.
B. Animated graphic captions: a tested editable template workflow with controls
   for word emphasis, color and animation where the host supports it.

SRT is a timing/text exchange route. It does not preserve rich animation or
provide portable control of all fonts, positioning and word highlighting.

Both caption output types are required. Start with a readable subtitle preset
and a simple word-highlight/pop graphic preset, with editable design controls.
Validate English and Hinglish separately, including mixed-language sentences,
names and consistent Roman-script Hindi spelling. Keep correction and timing
alignment separate so a spelling correction does not silently move sound cues.
Do not assume that one transcription model is accurate for every language.

3. YOUR SFX LIBRARY

Connect D:\Asset\SFX recursively, preserving full relative paths and nested
folder context. A read-only inventory found 17 nested folders, including
SIGNAL's sound-family subfolders and Whoosh Sfx Pack\02-Whoosh Sfx Pack.
The recursive audit contains 522 audio-container files, 19 video-container
files and 3 other/hidden files. There are 540 audio-bearing files: one video
has no audio stream. Per-file technical results are saved in library-audit.
Build a local catalog containing:
- Original path, display name, folders, user tags and favorites.
- Duration, sample rate, channel count and audio-preview information.
- Duplicate identity and availability so moved/offline files can be identified.
- An optional preferred sync point: file start, audible onset or impact peak.

Start retrieval with filenames and folder context as a first filter, then use
audio evidence from the requested Real SFX and Earshot skills. Real SFX measures
attack, decay, audible duration, spectral character and channel behavior. Earshot
provides local semantic audio matching and ranked descriptions with measured
evidence. Allow searches such as 'short soft pop', 'deep impact' or 'rising whoosh'.
AI maps instructions to sound descriptions and queries the measured catalog.
Poorly named files remain searchable by their audio content. Model descriptions
and similarity scores are provisional evidence; they do not establish taste,
recording provenance, licensing, or suitability for a particular edit.

For long recordings and bundled collections, search candidate segments and check
their starts/tails before use. Whole-file semantic tags sample long recordings
and do not certify every sound inside them. Keep chosen source ranges and their
onset/arrival/peak anchor in the placement plan. Use only your connected library;
disable fallback UI packs and generated sound for this workflow.

Earshot does not transcribe spoken words. English/Hinglish caption transcription
requires a separate transcription/alignment component; sound analysis cannot
replace it. Reviewing sync, masking and pacing requires an actual video or mix,
so library analysis alone must not be presented as a finished sound-design review.

Show an audition button, duration and file location for each result. Let you
replace a sound after automatic placement or optionally review first. Preserve
the original files. Check video containers for audio; visual overlays without
audio must not be treated as sound effects. Track bundled collections as
source segments where necessary, rather than placing a complete compilation.

4. SFX PLACEMENT

Supported cue types to build first:
- A chosen effect at the playhead or an explicit sequence time.
- A sound anchored to a selected word, caption entrance, marker or cut.
- A sound ending at a transition or an impact peak aligned to the cue.

The placement plan records the library file, source trim, sync point, sequence
time, destination track, level and optional fades. A whoosh's start may precede
the target cut; an impact file may contain leading silence. Align the intended
audible event rather than blindly aligning every file's start.

Use dedicated SFX tracks and explicit overlap rules. Placement should preserve
the picture edit. Provide density limits, minimum spacing, repetition avoidance
and favorites/exclusions as recipe preferences. Do not add one sound per word
unless that is the requested style.

Level controls distinguish relative gain changes from absolute values. Compare
against dialogue during playback and meter the combined result. 'Quiet' is a
creative preference, not a universal fixed gain value.

Sound-design analysis requested by you:
- Real SFX provides library measurement, source-segment and arrival/peak placement
  rules, dialogue-preservation checks and a critique workflow.
- Earshot provides local calibrated semantic matching and an independent AudioSet
  cross-check for representative candidates, plus objective audio measurements.
- Keep one persistent local analysis worker and cache audio features/embeddings;
  do not reload the models or re-analyse every file for each caption or command.
- A caption entrance alone does not require a sound. Use the instruction's
  emphasis and density rules; reserve larger accents for selected moments.
- Library analysis is complete only to its reported coverage. For a real edit,
  verify chosen source ranges, alignment, dialogue masking, headroom and mono
  playback. Do not substitute a launch-film scoring profile for your taste.
- Prefer editable individual SFX clips in Premiere. A separate SFX stem/export
  can be an optional handoff, rather than flattening all placements by default.

5. TWO OPERATING MODES

Suggestion mode: the tool proposes caption changes and sound cues, lets you
audition/replace candidates and applies the accepted plan.

Automatic recipe mode: it places sounds under explicit saved rules, then shows
the resulting placements for review and replacement.

Automatic recipe mode is your chosen default. A single Run instruction starts
the supported operation; individual sound placements do not require repeated
approval. Review, replace and recovery remain available afterward. Suggestion
mode is optional. If no suitable sound can be identified, leave the cue unfilled
and explain it rather than inventing a library file or using an arbitrary sound.

6. PREMIERE INTEGRATION: WHAT MUST BE PROVEN

Adobe documents transcript exchange/transcription and sequence media placement
through UXP [1], [2]. These are building blocks for the design, not evidence
that the complete workflow already works on your installed release.

CaptionTrack's documented controls do not establish full caption text/style
editing [3]. Test native-caption generation/import and style application early.
Where the host API cannot complete a step, provide an explicit native import
step or a supported alternative; label it accurately in the workflow [4].

Do not equate inserting a MOGRT with being able to update every template's text
and word styling. The documented MogrtText class is marked Since 27.0, so that
specific interface is not a valid baseline for your 26.2 installation [5].
Animated captions need an early proof on 26.2 using the exact template and
connector. Evaluate a supported alternative adapter if necessary. If no editable
route passes, discuss the remaining manual step before expanding the feature.

Likewise, Adobe's current help describes native single-word captions [6], but
current help does not prove availability in 26.2 or complete plugin control.

Compatibility plan:
- 26.2.0 on your Windows machine is the first integration target.
- UXP is the modern connector; selected older hosts may use CEP/ExtendScript.
- Caption output capabilities and SFX placement are tested independently.
- The transcript/cue/recipe data stays independent of Premiere's connector.
- A published build lists exact tested versions and any manual import steps.
- Support for every version with identical features is not promised.

7. FIRST RELEASE AND BUILD ORDER

Stage A - Prove both core integrations before polishing the panel.
Use a short sequence to obtain timed dialogue, deliver editable captions through
the chosen route, and place one local sound on an SFX track at a word/cut.
Verify frame alignment, text editability, audio level controls and recovery.
Prove the exact animated template now, because both caption styles are required.

Stage B - Caption editor.
Implement transcript correction, grouping/timing, English/Hinglish handling,
one standard subtitle preset and one animated graphic preset, preview and the
tested Premiere delivery routes. Add more styles after both complete workflows
work. Roman-script Hindi is a provisional display default, not a transcription
quality guarantee.

Stage C - Library browser.
Scan D:\Asset\SFX and every nested folder, index filenames/tags/metadata,
audition and search, and insert a chosen result at an explicit cue. Include
audio-bearing video containers only through a tested audio-only import route.
Resolve exact duplicates and collection segments. Confirm imported files by path.

Stage D - AI cue planning.
Use your instructions to propose where sounds belong and which library entries
fit. Connect caption keywords and edit markers to the same timing map.
Automatically place matching sounds using your instructions and saved density,
repetition, timing and level rules. Offer replacements and reusable recipes.

Stage E - Compatibility and packaging.
Run the caption and SFX workflow on selected older hosts. Package the supported
connectors and companion with setup instructions and exact feature limitations.

8. ACCEPTANCE SCENARIO

On an agreed short test sequence:
- Caption text is correct after your review and stays aligned to the dialogue.
- The chosen caption style can be edited as advertised after delivery.
- Search retrieves your known library examples and auditions the right file.
- Every inserted effect comes from an existing file in the connected library.
- Word/cut cues align to the intended onset/peak within one sequence frame.
- Placement leaves picture timing intact and respects the target track policy.
- Replacing a sound preserves the cue's intended alignment.
- Re-running a recipe detects its existing placements instead of duplicating them.
- Moving a source file, changing the edit or canceling a job produces a clear
  recoverable state, rather than a false completion message.
- Playback and metering confirm that SFX levels suit the agreed dialogue mix.
- The same declared features pass on each claimed supported Premiere build.

automatic placement from your own library, and recursive folder coverage.

Hinglish is a provisional default that can be changed without redesigning the tool.

measurements now cover all 540 audio-bearing files, including audio inside video
containers. Earshot scanned 537 unique audio contents (exact copies reuse their
evidence); 530 file entries have descriptions and 10 effectively silent entries

still needs its integration proof; this analysis does not certify API support.
